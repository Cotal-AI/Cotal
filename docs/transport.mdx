Cotal runs on NATS and JetStream. Its wire contract defines who a message reaches,
who may read it, and what survives a disconnect. NATS provides the routing and storage
mechanisms that the current implementation uses to meet those guarantees.

This distinction matters when you build a client or consider another transport.
A client for today's binding uses NATS directly. Another binding would have to preserve
the coordination guarantees as well as carry the message bytes.

## The wire contract [#the-two-layers]

The Cotal protocol is the normative wire contract in the [SPEC](../SPEC.md).
It defines message shapes, space and channel addressing, delivery modes, presence,
discovery, history, and version rules. Sender identity and delivery class come from
the delivery path. A payload cannot turn a channel post into a direct message or
claim a different sender.

A transport binding gives that contract a concrete encoding and implementation.
[SPEC §3](../SPEC.md#3-subject-layout) defines the current subject grammar, and
[§8 through §10](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding) define the NATS binding and its
authorization rules. Those rules are normative for clients using this binding.
The TypeScript reference client implements them in
[`subjects.ts`](../packages/core/src/subjects.ts),
[`types.ts`](../packages/core/src/types.ts), and the generated
[`cotal.schema.json`](../spec/cotal.schema.json).

There is no pluggable transport interface or second transport binding in the current
implementation. The distinction between contract and binding describes what a future
implementation must preserve. [Architecture](architecture.mdx) explains how the
current packages divide that work.

## Required transport capabilities [#the-transport-capability-contract]

A deployment needs all five capabilities below. If a transport lacks one, a binding
must supply the missing behavior above it.

| Capability | Guarantee to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| Addressed routing | Hierarchical channel names and wildcard subscriptions. Multicast reaches channel subscribers, unicast addresses one instance, and anycast assigns work to one holder of a role. Control requests can address services. The delivery path attributes the sender and delivery class. |
| Durable delivery and history | At-least-once delivery within retention for unicast, anycast, and durable-channel backstops. Per-instance pending state and per-role queued work survive disconnects. Receivers acknowledge after surfacing or handling a copy, tolerate duplicates by message id, and support bounded history replay. Live channel copies remain at-most-once. |
| Presence and registry state | Per-space presence records with own-key writes, liveness derived from TTL expiry, stale timestamps, or deletion, and persistent channel configuration. |
| Identity | An authenticated principal identifies an instance across reconnects. Managed lifecycle identity distinguishes a same-name successor from its predecessor. |
| Authorization and isolation | A space confines its participants. An agent publishes as itself, posts only within its default-deny `allowPublish` ACL, reads its own DMs, and reads channel content only within `allowSubscribe`. Durable delivery also checks lifecycle and membership. |

Routing a live message does not provide durable delivery or a presence registry.
An offline member needs retained messages and a pending cursor. An observer needs
stored presence records and rules for deciding when a record no longer means online.
[Presence and delivery](presence-and-delivery.mdx) explains those guarantees.

## The NATS binding [#nats-reference-binding]

The reference implementation combines native NATS mechanisms with Cotal logic.
NATS enforces subject grants and isolates accounts. JetStream retains messages,
tracks consumer acknowledgements, redelivers pending copies, and stores KV records.
Cotal chooses the authorized recipients, manages lifecycle and membership state,
derives presence, and controls when a received copy is surfaced or acknowledged.

### Subject routing

The messaging subjects encode their addresses and sender principals:

```text
cotal.<space>.chat.<owner>.<actor>.<channel>
cotal.<space>.inst.<recipientOwner>.<recipientActor>.<senderOwner>.<senderActor>
cotal.<space>.svc.<role>.<owner>.<actor>
```

The reference client's `parseSubject` recovers the sender and delivery class from
these subjects. `*` matches one subject token, and a final `>` matches one or more
remaining tokens. A publisher names one concrete channel, while a subscriber can
name a channel subtree. Typed commands use the separate
[endpoint control subjects](../SPEC.md#132-grammar), including service-addressed
requests and replies. The [subject layout](../SPEC.md#3-subject-layout) defines each
position, and [Spaces](spaces.mdx) explains the channel boundary.

### Durable delivery

JetStream captures messaging publishes in `CHAT_<space>`, `DM_<space>`, and
`TASK_<space>`. A live channel subscriber receives the same publish through a native
core-NATS subscription bounded by `sub.allow`. That live copy has no acknowledgement.
For a durable channel, the [delivery daemon](delivery-daemon.mdx) writes per-member
backstop copies and rechecks the current read ACL, membership interval, and lifecycle
before handing content to a member through `dlv_<owner>-<actor>-<uid>`.

An authenticated agent binds its provisioned `dm_<owner>-<actor>-<uid>` consumer for
DMs. Holders of a role share the `svc_<role>` consumer for queued anycast work.
The role queue remains shared when its holders change. Per-instance pending state
belongs to a lifecycle. A successor with the same principal starts after its own
activation frontier and inherits neither its predecessor's backlog nor the inactive
gap. [SPEC §8](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding) defines these intervals and the
consumer policies.

Publishers set `Nats-Msg-Id` to the message id for native publish deduplication.
Receivers still need duplicate-tolerant handlers because at-least-once delivery can
redeliver. Cotal acknowledges a consumed copy only after it is surfaced or handled.
An empty id supplies no publish-deduplication key, but a consumed copy still needs
its own acknowledgement. History and late-join backfill use short-lived,
single-channel `chathist_<owner>-<actor>-<uid>` consumers pinned to `allowSubscribe`.
Agents have no unfiltered Direct Get grant. See the
[acknowledgement and history rules](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding) and NATS's
[pull consumer documentation](https://docs.nats.io/learn/jetstream/pull-consumers).

### Presence storage

`cotal_presence_<space>` stores presence with a 6000 ms TTL, and
`cotal_channels_<space>` stores channel configuration without a TTL.
Cotal publishes heartbeats and derives offline state from record deletion or stale
liveness. KV supplies the records and watches.
The derived `cotal_membership_<space>` feed combines observed live subscriptions and
durable membership for display. That feed is not delivery or authorization authority.
The broker's subscribe ACL and the privileged membership registry remain authoritative.
See [SPEC §6](../SPEC.md#6-presence-and-discovery) and
[§8](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding).

### Space isolation

The principal `owner.actor` is `AgentCard.id`, the subject sender, `from.id`, and the
presence key. Durable names use its unambiguous dash-form `owner-actor` and add a
`lifecycleUid` for per-instance state. A reconnect preserves the principal.
A same-name successor has a different lifecycle UID. A supervised restart or process
takeover preserves the UID and advances `processEpoch` to fence the old process.
The connection nkey authenticates the transport and scopes the per-connection reply
inbox, rather than defining the principal. The
[identity rules](../SPEC.md#2-identity) and
[lifecycle rules](../SPEC.md#131-lifecycle-identity) distinguish these scopes.

Each authenticated space has an operator-signed NATS account. Profile-specific JWT
ACLs constrain subjects, consumer operations, and other resources inside that account.
The reference implementation builds these grants in
[`provision.ts`](../packages/core/src/provision.ts) from shared subject and stream
builders. Open development mode is outside these security claims.
[Identity and authentication](identity-and-auth.mdx),
[SPEC §9](../SPEC.md#9-nats--jetstream-security-and-authorization), and
[Appendix B](../SPEC.md#appendix-b-profile-acls) define the boundaries.

### Client lifecycle details

The reference client uses native heartbeat detection and manages the ordered consumers
it creates for scans and watches. These are client API contracts rather than extra
capabilities a replacement binding must copy:

- [Native transport liveness](embedding-exports.mdx#native-transport-liveness) describes the paired heartbeat options and resident-socket behavior.
- [Finite KV scans](embedding-exports.mdx#finite-kv-scans) describes completion, cancellation, and cleanup limits.
- [Delivery lease watches](embedding-daemons.mdx#delivery-lease-watches) describes cleanup during stop, reconnect, replacement, and bind races.

<Callout type="info" title="Broker requirements">

The endpoint control binding requires `nats-server >= 2.12` and native message
schedules, with no degraded fallback. Cotal also uses per-message TTLs for bounded
records. NATS's [2.12 upgrade guide](https://docs.nats.io/release-notes/upgrade-to-2.12)
documents the scheduling feature.
[SPEC §13.12](../SPEC.md#1312-nats--jetstream-binding) defines the complete broker
requirements, including atomic create-CAS, counters, and deployment-specific
`max_control_line` and `max_payload` floors. Cotal assumes 2.12 schedule replacement
semantics, rather than requiring the later stop-plus-publish behavior.

</Callout>

## Another transport binding [#binding-to-another-transport]

A future binding must account for each capability above. A transport with native
routing and authentication might still lack retained messages, consumer cursors,
presence records, or lifecycle fencing. The binding would need to supply those
behaviors and preserve their authorization checks.

For example, adding replay to a live-only transport requires retained content and
per-recipient pending state. A shared inbox also needs a trusted way to recheck current
read permission before releasing content. Replacing NATS therefore changes more than
the connection code. Building a client for the existing binding has a smaller scope.
See [Build a client](build-a-client.mdx).

## Implications for implementers [#what-this-means]

Use the normative wire contract when implementing a client. Its message schemas,
addressing, delivery guarantees, presence, channel semantics, and change rules describe
the behavior peers depend on. The current NATS binding specifies how to realize that
behavior today.

The project keeps NATS as its reference binding and defers a pluggable transport
interface until a second binding has a consumer. Any portability claim must account
for durable delivery and registry state, including both the native JetStream
mechanisms and the Cotal logic that enforces the contract above them.
