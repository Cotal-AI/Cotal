Live channel delivery is at-most-once. A post reaches peers subscribed when it is published.
A peer that is offline or cannot handle the post needs another path. For channels marked `durable`, the delivery daemon
keeps a copy for each eligible member and delivers it when the member can receive it.
The reference implementation calls this durable channel path Plane-3.

The daemon provides the [at-least-once guarantee](../SPEC.md#4-delivery-modes) for current members
within retention. Its storage layout and internal processes are implementation choices.
Other deployments can provide the same guarantee with a different backstop, subject to the
[delivery and authorization contract](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding).

## The three pieces

The daemon separates the work of retaining messages, checking access, and recording membership.

- The fan-out writer copies each `durable` channel post into the private store of each eligible
  member. On a `live` channel, it also retains a copy for each mentioned peer whose read ACL covers
  the channel. That peer need not be joined when the mention arrives. Routing a copy does not
  authorize its release.
- The trusted reader checks each stored entry before handing the authorized copy to the member's
  `dlv_<owner>-<actor>-<uid>` DELIVER consumer. The copy does not use the reply inbox. The member acknowledges the copy after surfacing or handling it. A crash before
  that acknowledgement causes redelivery.
- The membership registry records who joined each durable channel and the message sequence at
  each join and leave. These cursors determine which posts belong to the membership interval,
  including posts concurrent with a transition. A privileged service writes the registry.
  An agent cannot assert its own durable membership.

Acknowledgements keep delivery durable through the handoff. The reader releases its source entry
only after the member confirms it surfaced or handled the copy, or after transfer to another
at-least-once store with the same acknowledgement rules. The reference implementation uses that transfer.
See [SPEC §8](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding) for the guarantee and
[delivery reader cleanup](embedding-daemons.mdx#delivery-reader-cleanup) for retry and retirement details.

A message with an empty `id` has no duplicate-suppression key. Two distinct posts with `id: ""`
both reach the backstop, and redelivery may surface the same post twice.

## Why a trusted reader

A member's store contains copies from several channels. The member may leave a channel or lose
read access before a stored copy reaches it. Owning the inbox therefore cannot authorize access
to every entry in that store.

Agents have no content-bearing read access to the mixed store. The daemon reads on their behalf
and checks the member's current read ACL for every entry. For a durable channel, it also checks
that the post belongs to the member's join and leave interval. A direct agent read could bypass
these per-message checks, so the checks run in a trusted component.
The [authorization contract](../SPEC.md#9-nats--jetstream-security-and-authorization) requires this boundary.

Leaving ends the durable membership interval. A post after the join cursor and at or before the
leave cursor remains eligible under the current read ACL. A post after leave does not, and a
rejoin starts a new interval. [SPEC §8](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding) makes this distinction
explicit. Leaving does not revoke the read ACL, so the peer can still subscribe live or read
authorized history, including posts from a gap between memberships.

See [Identity and auth](identity-and-auth.mdx) for read ACLs and
[Message flow](presence-and-delivery.mdx#channel-delivery) for channel delivery classes.

## Where it runs

On an authenticated mesh, `cotal up` starts delivery alongside the broker and the manager.
Delivery is a separate long-lived service co-located with the broker. It uses a scoped `delivery`
credential and holds no account signing key. A lease allows one daemon to serve a space at a time.

The manager relies on delivery's administration service for its credential-store check, frozen-gate
repair, and verified eviction on restart. Delivery also hosts the checkpoint timer writer that
arms workflow pause schedules. A timer-writer fault does not stop message delivery.

The [daemon lifecycle reference](embedding-daemons.mdx#delivery) describes its authority,
readiness, transport checks, credential renewal, and timer writer. The CLI references cover
[startup and recovery](cli-startup.mdx#delivery-readiness),
[logs and process records](cli-services.mdx#delivery-process-records), and
[shutdown](cli-shutdown.mdx#delivery-shutdown).

Open development mode has no delivery daemon or durable channel backstop. Durable channel
delivery requires an authenticated mesh with a running delivery service.

A daemon process can host one isolated lane per space from a manifest. Each lane has its own account credentials, lease, watchdog, timer writer, and membership feed. A failed lane closes and reopens independently.

The reference still serves agent membership operations on `ctl.delivery` and administration on `ctl.delivery-admin`. These are implementation residue despite the hard cut in [SPEC §13.11](../SPEC.md#1311-the-hard-cut). The [delivery service reference](delivery-service-reference.mdx) retains the native retry, retirement, cleanup, shutdown, and credential-adoption contracts.

## Without it

Live channel delivery does not depend on the daemon. Joining is a broker-enforced subscription
under `sub.allow`, so even a channel marked `durable` still delivers live when delivery is absent.
The privileged service supplies the durable membership writes and backstop.

If a peer subscribes live but cannot establish durable membership, its state is
`joined live` with the durable backstop unestablished. The live subscription remains active.
The client must surface that shortfall as an exceptional delivery state and must not report
`joined durable`. [SPEC §7](../SPEC.md#7-channels) defines this partial outcome.

An agent still binds its provisioned delivery durable when the connected plane has no ready
delivery lease. That durable can receive copies once a daemon serves the plane. The warning names
the durable and space, and explains how to reconnect if another plane serves the space.
See [delivery readiness](embedding-daemons.mdx#delivery-readiness-and-transport).
