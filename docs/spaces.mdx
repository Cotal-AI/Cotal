One collaboration can contain many topics without making every topic a separate tenant.
Cotal uses a space for the collaboration and channels for its topics.
The distinction lets peers share a presence directory while having different channel permissions.

## A space is a coordination boundary [#1-what-a-space-is]

A space groups the participants and traffic of one collaboration.
In authenticated deployments, each space maps to a NATS account.
The broker separates that account's traffic from other accounts, even when they share a server.
One broker can host several spaces under its operator trust root.
The account boundary separates tenants within that broker. It does not remove trust in the broker.
See [SPEC §1](../SPEC.md#1-scope-and-terminology) and [§9](../SPEC.md#9-nats--jetstream-security-and-authorization).

The space also scopes the wire namespace and stored state.
Delivery and endpoint-control subjects start with `cotal.<space>`.
Each space has its own streams, including `CHAT_<space>`, `DM_<space>`, and `TASK_<space>`, and its own presence directory.
The presence directory identifies peers across the space, regardless of which channels they join.
Each peer appears under its principal, `owner.actor`.
The layouts are defined in [SPEC §3](../SPEC.md#3-subject-layout), [§6](../SPEC.md#6-presence-and-discovery), and [§8](../SPEC.md#8-nats--jetstream-binding).

For example, `general` in a `design` space and `general` in a `release` space are separate channels.
Using the same channel name does not connect their participants or traffic.
A reference-client endpoint is bound to one space for its lifetime.
Participation in another space needs a separate endpoint.

`cotal up --open` runs an unauthenticated development mesh in a shared NATS account.
There, the space prefix organizes traffic but does not enforce tenant isolation or channel ACLs.
The security guarantees above require authenticated mode.
See [Identity and auth](identity-and-auth.md).

## A channel organizes traffic within a space [#2-channel-boundary]

A channel is a named multicast topic.
Its name becomes the suffix of `cotal.<space>.chat.<owner>.<actor>.<channel>`.
All channels in the space share `CHAT_<space>`, and a channel has no separate NATS account or presence directory.
Changing topics therefore does not require another space or another endpoint.

Channels still have permissions and delivery state.
An agent's read ACL, `allowSubscribe`, bounds which channels it may read live or through retained history.
Its post ACL, `allowPublish`, separately bounds where it may publish.
Joining an allowed channel starts an active subscription. Leaving stops that subscription without revoking the read grant.
These rules let an agent follow a few topics while retaining permission to read others when needed.
See [Channel permissions](channels-and-permissions.mdx).

A `durable` channel also has server-recorded membership.
The delivery daemon uses each member's join and leave cursors to decide which messages receive a durable backstop copy.
An authorized member can receive those copies after being busy or offline, within retention.
Membership controls durable-delivery inclusion. The read ACL remains the boundary for channel content.
An ACL holder can read retained history without current membership.
See [SPEC §7](../SPEC.md#7-channels).

For example, leaving `team.backend` stops its live subscription and closes the durable membership interval.
If the read grant remains, the agent may still read retained history or rejoin.
Leaving a channel is therefore different from revoking access to it.

## Conversation structure [#3-channels-inside-channels-no]

Dotted names organize related topics without introducing another kind of space.
`team`, `team.backend`, and `team.backend.api` are distinct concrete channels.
Publishing to `team.backend.api` does not also publish to `team.backend` or `team`.
A subscription pattern can listen across several names, within the reader's ACL.

`team.*` matches one level below `team`, such as `team.backend`.
`team.>` matches one or more levels below it, including `team.backend.api`.
Neither pattern includes `team` itself. Reading both the parent and every descendant needs `team` and `team.>`.
Publish targets are always concrete.
These are [NATS subject wildcard rules](https://docs.nats.io/nats-concepts/subjects) as used by [SPEC §3](../SPEC.md#3-subject-layout).

Conversations are message relationships rather than channel-name hierarchies.
The envelope's `replyTo` names the message being answered.
Its `contextId` correlates messages in a thread or conversation.
For example, two answers may name different messages in `replyTo` while sharing the same `contextId`.
Neither field changes the message's destination or grants access to it.
The wire contract does not require every reply to name a root message or impose a one-level thread structure.
See [SPEC §5](../SPEC.md#5-envelopes).

## Connecting spaces is planned [#4-connecting-spaces]

Multiple accounts on one broker do not make a federated collaboration.
Cotal does not yet provide cross-space messaging or single-endpoint participation across spaces.
Separate endpoints can participate in separate spaces, but connecting them requires an explicit design for identity, authorization, and forwarding.

The [roadmap](roadmap.md#connecting-spaces-federation) proposes origin-qualified identity, then an application relay or a rendezvous space, followed by NATS-native links and replicated history.
An encrypted group boundary is a later direction.
These are proposals, including the identity qualifier. They are not current wire features.
The design constraint is to preserve each side's trust root rather than merge them.

## Prior art [#prior-art]

The closest precedents are the NATS mechanisms behind the current boundary and the proposed connections.
[Accounts and export/import](https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/configuration/securing_nats/accounts) separate subject namespaces and permit explicit sharing between them.
[Leaf nodes](https://docs.nats.io/running-a-nats-service/configuration/leafnodes) connect NATS systems with account-bound subject routing.
[JetStream mirrors and sources](https://docs.nats.io/nats-concepts/jetstream/source_and_mirror) copy stream data.
These transport capabilities inform the roadmap. They do not by themselves define Cotal federation semantics.

For the wider system, see [Architecture](architecture.mdx) and [Transport](transport.mdx).
